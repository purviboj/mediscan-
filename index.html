Project Proposal

Hands-free CT navigation built for sterile operating rooms.

Surgeons should not pause mid-procedure to ask someone else to scroll through scans. Medi-Scan uses computer vision and gesture recognition to let the surgeon control CT images directly, without touching a screen and without breaking sterility.

Problem

One small interruption inside surgery creates a large operational cost.

During procedures, surgeons often rely on nurses or technicians to manipulate CT scans on nearby workstations. That creates friction at the exact moment speed and focus matter.

Time loss

Repeated verbal requests slow image review during critical decisions.

Broken flow

Attention shifts from anatomy to coordination with support staff.

Sterility risk

Any workaround that involves contact adds avoidable contamination pressure.

Adoption gap

Existing touchless systems are often too expensive or too cumbersome to deploy.

Solution

Medi-Scan turns hand gestures into surgical imaging commands.

Gesture Navigation

Swipe through image slices, rotate the view, or freeze movement with a palm-up hold.

Sterile by Design

No touch, no glove changes, and no dependence on an intermediary during scan review.

Fast Training

Simple gestures reduce onboarding time and support pilot adoption inside hospital workflows.

Lower Cost

Computer vision software on commodity hardware positions the system below existing alternatives.

Value Proposition

Medi-Scan allows surgeons to control imaging hands-free during surgery, improving patient safety, efficiency, and adoption speed at a fraction of the cost of existing solutions.

Pilot Market

Start with New Jersey hospital systems where OR efficiency and innovation budgets align.

The first pilot focus is New Jersey, where regional health systems can validate sterile workflow gains, staff usability, and procurement readiness before broader expansion.

Pilot buyer Hospital innovation, surgery, and perioperative operations leaders
Pilot setting High-imaging surgical specialties with frequent CT review moments
Success metric Reduced verbal handoffs, faster image access, and strong surgeon preference

Phases of the Plan

Structured rollout from prototype to hospital deployment.

Phase 1

Prototype

Build the CT viewer interaction model, define gesture vocabulary, and validate usability with mock scans.

Phase 2

Technical Validation

Improve detection accuracy, tune latency, and test gesture performance under OR lighting and positioning constraints.

Phase 3

Clinical Pilot

Run a New Jersey hospital pilot with surgeon feedback loops, workflow metrics, and compliance documentation.

Phase 4

Procurement Readiness

Package ROI evidence, integration requirements, training materials, and deployment support for hospital adoption.

Phase 5

Scale

Expand to additional specialties and health systems with product refinements informed by pilot outcomes.

Revenue Model

Revenue architecture and pricing strategy aligned with hospital procurement.

1) Hardware License (Entry Fee)

One-time capital sale of camera and compute unit per OR suite: $18,000-$25,000 per unit.

2) SaaS Platform Subscription (Recurring)

Annual software license for model updates, cloud sync, and compliance tools: $8,400 per suite per year.

3) Clinical Training and Onboarding

Credentialing program and simulation lab integration: $2,500-$5,000 per cohort.

4) Pricing Positioning

Positioned as a lower-cost alternative to robotic systems, with messaging around dramatic cost delta versus systems near $1M.
